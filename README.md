# Caribbean ICT Research — Patents, IP, and the PASS Model

**Status:** Complete — submitted as academic coursework
**Type:** Research and policy analysis
**Context:** Ethics in Computing assignment, University of Guyana

---

## Overview

This repository documents two connected research pieces examining technology, intellectual property, and digital development in the Caribbean context — with a particular focus on Guyana.

---

## Research Piece 1 — Patents and IP in the Caribbean Tech Landscape

### The Question

How does the global intellectual property framework affect technology access, innovation, and development in small Caribbean nations — and what does a specific software patent look like when examined in detail?

### Approach

The assignment used SAP SE's US Patent 8,644,755 B2 as the focal case — a software patent from one of the world's largest enterprise technology companies. By examining a real, active patent from a company that operates in the Caribbean market, the analysis moves beyond abstract IP theory into the concrete legal and economic realities facing Caribbean organisations that use, build on, or compete with enterprise software.

### Key Areas Examined

What software patents protect and how that protection works in practice. The relationship between patent holders and organisations in developing markets. How IP frameworks designed for large technology markets interact with smaller economies. The implications for local software development and technology entrepreneurship in Guyana and the wider Caribbean.

---

## Research Piece 2 — The PASS Model and Caribbean Educational Technology

### Context

This research connects to a broader thesis project (ITE4200) examining technology integration in Guyanese education — specifically the PASS Model framework as applied to the Caribbean educational context.

### Key Areas Examined

How existing educational technology frameworks translate (or fail to translate) to Caribbean classroom realities. The policy environment shaping ICT adoption in Guyanese schools. The gap between technology availability and pedagogically effective technology use.

---

## Why This Research Is in a Tech Portfolio

Understanding the policy and legal environment that shapes technology is part of being an effective technology practitioner — not just an academic exercise. A data analyst or automation specialist working in the Caribbean context needs to understand why certain software tools are available or unavailable in the region, how IP constraints affect the tools organisations can build or license, and what the policy landscape looks like for organisations trying to adopt or implement technology.

This research builds that contextual understanding.
